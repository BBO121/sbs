Are the disposable plastics we thoughtlessly used for the sake of convenience during trips with children really 'convenient'? Behind that brief comfort lie hundreds of thousands of microplastics flowing into our children's bodies. If you are concerned about your child's health, disposable plastic might actually be the most 'inconvenient and unsettling' substance of all.

    If the plastic used for the sake of temporary comfort is accumulating in a child's body, wouldn't a plastic-free environment sometimes be much better for the child?

[부제 - 01] 
Q. You usually go on 'random trips' with your daughter Hara, but this time you went on a special 'Plastic Detox Challenge Trip.' I am curious about why you chose to go on a 'Plastic Detox Challenge' trip and what preparations you made for it. 

    [문단 - 01]
    A. There were times when Hara would often tell me stories about environmental pollution after coming home from kindergarten. I also realized, while traveling, that I use more single-use plastic than I thought. <b>So, I became curious: 'Is it really possible to travel without plastic?'</b> 

    [문단 - 02]
    Before the trip, I watched documentaries about the dangers of plastic with Hara, and I packed a tumbler, handkerchief, lunch box, and shopping bag in advance. <b>I wanted to naturally show my child, who was traveling with me, that protecting the environment while traveling is not something difficult or grandiose.</b>

[이미지 - 02 설명 caption]
Packing for a plastic detox while setting off on a random turtle trip with Hara / Photo courtesy of Harapapa
 
[부제 - 02]
Q. What were the most rewarding and challenging moments you experienced while traveling plastic-free?

    [문단 - 01]
    A. What surprised me most was that I was able to reduce my use of single-use plastics with less effort than I had anticipated. I used a reusable lunch box when buying food at the market, and I was satisfied because it not only reduced plastic packaging but also allowed me to pack my food more neatly and hygienically. The same went for tumblers. At first, I thought carrying one around might be a hassle, but once I actually used it, I found it kept food hot or cold better and was actually quite convenient in many ways. However, I did feel a sense of disappointment when I saw most water bottles in convenience stores. While it is still not easy to completely avoid plastic, <b>I felt that simply reducing our plastic use little by little whenever we have the choice is meaningful.</b>

[부제 - 03]
Q. I am curious to know what this Plastic Detox Challenge trip meant to Hara. Did you notice any changes in Hara through this trip?

    [문단 - 01]
    A. Even after returning from a trip, Hara sometimes says, “Dad, this is plastic. We shouldn’t use plastic!” Whenever she says that, I find it cute, and I feel proud that she is aware of the environment on her own. Since she is still young, I don’t think it is important for her to practice it perfectly. However, I think the experience of thinking a little more—asking questions like, “What kind of material is this?” or “Do we really have to use this?”—serves as good education in itself. I also think that if she encounters plastic-polluted oceans or animals affected by them in the future, it will be an even deeper learning experience for her.

[부제 - 04] 
Q. I am also curious to know how Mr. Hara's father felt about 'single-use plastics' through this trip, both as a traveler and as a parent. Did your personal views change as well?

    [문단 - 01]
    A. At first, I wondered, "Will the world really change just because I use a little less plastic?" However, after trying it myself, I realized that reducing plastic isn't as difficult or inconvenient as I thought. In fact, there were moments when using a tumbler or reusable containers was actually more convenient. While achieving 100% plastic-free status immediately might be difficult, I felt that gradually reducing usage within my means is entirely possible. Since then, I have started carrying a tumbler more often in my daily life. Although it is a small step, I developed a desire to do things one by one that I can for the environment my child will live in.

[부제 - 05]
Q. You even decorated the Plastic Detox Challenge Calendar yourself with Hara. Would this challenge calendar be helpful for those who want to practice a plastic detox with their children?

    [문단 - 01]
    A. I felt it was a great tool to actually put into practice with my child. Hara even asked, "Dad, did I use a lot of plastic today?" while looking at the calendar in the morning. It was fascinating to see how the record remained in a visible form, prompting the child to reflect on it on their own and naturally continue the practice.

    [문단 - 02]
    I think the process of a child trying things out, marking them, and receiving praise is more important than just explaining environmental practices verbally. In that sense, I believe this calendar can help children and parents create small changes together.

[부제 - 06]
Q. July is 'Plastic Free July,' a global initiative to reduce plastic use. Please share a word of encouragement with those who, like Hara's dad, want to start a daily detox with their children or even alone, but lack the courage to take the plunge.

    [문단 - 01]
    A. Taking action for the environment doesn't seem as difficult or grandiose as you might think. Rather than feeling pressured to do it perfectly, I suggest starting with just one thing you can do today.

    [문단 - 02]
    I was naturally moved when I realized that even a small action can make the world our children will live in a little cleaner. As the easiest way, I would like to start by recommending the use of a tumbler. I tried it myself, and it kept my drinks cold for longer and was much more convenient than I expected. It may be a small change, but I hope we can get started together.

    [문단 - 03]
    Their journy, which began with the question "Will it really work?", led them to the conviction that even if not perfect, reducing plastic little by little is meaningful enough.

    July is 'Plastic Free July,' a global initiative to reduce plastic in our daily lives. For a healthy planet for our children and the health of our families, why not join us in practicing a plastic-free lifestyle this July?
